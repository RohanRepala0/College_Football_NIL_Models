Market value is what a roster would cost in revenue share at the CSC's A4 rates, with players ranked on public data from before the season. On-field value is how much of the team's rating its players explain, priced at the same market rate per rating point. It is worked out four times, against SP+, ESPN FPI, SRS and Elo; surplus is the median of the four, and the range shows how far the ratings disagree. These are market prices, not what schools actually pay.

How on-field value works

  1. Score every player on this season's production (usage, expected points added, tackles, sacks and the like), recruiting or transfer-portal rating, and class year.
  2. Turn players into position-group strength. Each group has starter slots (1 QB, 1 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE, 5 OL, 4 DL, 3 LB, 4 DB, 1 K, 1 P); the next players count 30%, and only the part of a score above a national replacement level counts.
  3. Regress each team rating on those strengths across all 138 FBS teams, with weights that can't go negative. That says how many rating points each group is worth. The player-score weights are chosen per rating by cross-validation.
  4. Price the points at the A4 market: total A4 market value divided by total A4 points, so a rating point costs about half a million dollars. One point is about 0.3 wins over a 12-game season.
  5. Surplus is on-field value minus market value. It averages zero across the 68 programs by construction, so it ranks programs against each other.

Ratings used

SP+
Bill Connelly's efficiency rating; early in the season it leans on a preseason projection that fades as games are played.
ESPN FPI
ESPN's predictive rating, also with a preseason prior.
SRS
Opponent-adjusted scoring margin, no prior.
Elo
Results-based, carried over from last season.

A check fit only on 2026 final scores, with no prior, was explained as well as SP+ and gave class year the same weight, so SP+'s preseason prior is not what drives the player weights. The full test is in the repository's reports/ratings_2026.md.

Limits

  1. Individual contracts are private. Player values are ranks placed on the CSC's published distributions, so only program totals are shown.
  2. Public data ranks players only moderately well; that uncertainty is the market-value range.
  3. Defense is explained less well than offense, and kickers and punters barely move any rating.
  4. Coaching, scheme and luck that the ratings pick up but player scores can't explain are not credited to anyone.
  5. The market view stops at week 8, where the CollegeFootballRatings2026 weekly files stop; the published ratings run through the latest week.
