How the curves work

  1. The CSC publishes up to five percentiles per cohort: the 10th, 25th, median, 75th and 90th.
  2. A curve runs through them on a scale of log dollars against the normal score of the percentile, straight between published points.
  3. Revenue share prints each cohort twice, with and without athletes paid $0. Those are one distribution, so the With $0 table adds points to the paid curve.
  4. Past the published 10th and 90th the curve continues on a lognormal slope. For A4 football revenue share and all football NIL, the slope past the 90th is calibrated so football's total fits the revenue-share cap and NIL Go's own totals, which makes the top much thinner. Answers out there are estimates.
  5. Equal neighbouring percentiles mean many athletes got the same amount, so an amount there gets a range.
